# Interview Flow

## Stage 1. Initial Framing

Goal: understand what the client wants to create.

- What do you want to get as the final result?
- Who is this project for?
- Why did this task appear now?
- What should change after the project is completed?
- How will you know that the result is successful?

## Stage 2. Context

Goal: understand the business situation and constraints.

- In what context will the result be used?
- Who makes decisions on the project?
- Which deadlines matter?
- Is there a budget range?
- What constraints are already known?

## Stage 3. Audience

Goal: describe the people the project is for.

- Who is the primary audience?
- What do they already know about you?
- What action should they take?
- What doubts might they have?
- What will be convincing for them?

## Stage 4. Desired Result

Goal: translate expectations into observable quality signals.

- What must be included in the final result?
- What counts as a good result?
- What counts as a weak result?
- Which quality signals matter?
- Which similar examples do you like?

## Stage 5. Style and Taste

Goal: unpack subjective words.

- What does "modern" mean to you?
- What does "premium" mean to you?
- Which visual choices feel weak?
- Which emotions should the result create?
- What level of boldness is acceptable?

## Stage 6. Scope

Goal: define the boundaries of work.

- What is included in the task?
- What should stay out of scope?
- Which materials already exist?
- Which materials need to be created?
- Who will give feedback?

## Stage 7. Acceptance

Goal: create acceptance criteria.

- By which signals will you accept the result?
- Who gives final approval?
- How many revision rounds are acceptable?
- Which changes should count as a new task?
- Which risks should be discussed in advance?
